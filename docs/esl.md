Architecture Specification: Epistemic Synthetic Language (ESL) v1.1

1. Structural Overview and Design Philosophy

Epistemic Synthetic Language (ESL) v1.1 is engineered not as a final semantic dogma, but as a rigorous "research scaffold" for the training of cognitive habits. This iteration represents a strategic migration from the analytic particle system of v0.3 to a synthetic, obligatory morphology. In v1.1, the "Pali-Root & Sanskrit-Morphology Engine" utilizes a Pali vocalic substrate for semantic roots—leveraging its traditional focus on conditioned experience—and Sanskrit-inspired inflectional mechanics for structural rigidity. This transition ensures that the omission of informational provenance is no longer a stylistic choice but a structural malformation, making "epistemic negligence" perceptually salient during the real-time processing of speech.

The architecture is governed by a "Scientific Epistemic Modesty" mandate. The language serves as a design tool to reduce conflation errors—specifically the systemic confusion between direct perception and secondary inference. By reframing cognitive claims as testable hypotheses, ESL v1.1 distinguishes the "lived experience" (phenomenological reality) from the "world" that the experience purports to describe. This modular rigidity forces the speaker to acknowledge the boundary between private internal states and the interpreted external causes thereof.

2. The 6-Slot Linear Morpheme Template

The structural core of ESL v1.1 is the fixed, obligatory morpheme sequence. This linear template reduces cognitive load by standardizing provenance retrieval and making "epistemic gaps" audible. In this synthetic engine, the analytic particles of v0.3 are compressed and inflected into specific morphological slots.

The 6-Slot Sequence

Slot    Component   Functional Specification
1   ROOT    Semantic Core / Atomic Content (Pali-derived).
2   ACCESS  Epistemic Access Mode (Privileged vs. Manifestational).
3   EVIDENCE    Provenance Layer (Source of information retrieval).
4   ASPECT  State Status (Temporal persistence and existence).
5   FRAME   Interpretive Context / Evaluation Rules.
6   ENDORSEMENT Confidence Tier (Commitment level/Probability).

Structural Synthesis: kodha-më-prat-jā-fi-che

To illustrate the engine's mechanics, consider the synthesis of kodha-më-prat-jā-fi-che:

* kodha (ROOT): Semantic core: "Anger."
* më (ACCESS): Privileged introspective access (internal state report).
* prat (EVIDENCE): The morphophonemic compression of the v0.3 mi particle; indicates a present experiential encounter.
* jā (ASPECT): Indicates current state status (persistence of the event).
* fi (FRAME): Experiential frame; designates the report as a "lived state."
* che (ENDORSEMENT): Full endorsement; commitment to the reality of the experience.

This synthetic unit distinguishes the sensation of anger (më) from the external cause of anger (në), which would require an entirely different evidence/access configuration.

3. Epistemic Access Class Reclassification

ESL v1.1 formally migrates from "Experiencer Case" to "Epistemic Access Class" to eliminate "mind-reading" fallacies. This distinction enforces a structural boundary between privileged internal states and manifestational external observations.

* -më (Privileged/Introspective Access): Reserved for internal states where the speaker has direct, non-observable access (e.g., "I feel").
* -në (Manifestational/External Access): Applied to states observed through external phenomena or inferred manifestations (e.g., "He appears").

Access Logic Gates

Scenario    Morpheme    Operational Requirement
"I am experiencing fear."   -më Target is a first-person, non-observable sensation.
"Gavin is angry."   -në Target is an external entity; access is limited to manifestations.
"The environment is cold."  -në Target is a shared external condition, not a private sensation.

This classification forces the speaker to distinguish between the "private theater" of the mind and the "public theater" of the external world.

4. The Evidence Tier: Provenance Logic Gates

The Evidence Tier functions as the "Provenance Layer," requiring a mandatory identification of the informational source. Before a word can be successfully compiled, the speaker must pass through specific internal logic gates.

Evidence Morphemes and Protocols

1. prat (Direct Encounter - derived from mi)
  * Logic Gate: "Does the cognitive stack contain a present sensory or introspective episode supporting this exact content?"
    * Failure Case: Applying prat to an unobserved cause (e.g., "The pipe burst" when only water is visible).
    2. me (Memory)
      * Logic Gate: "Is the current basis for the assertion a retrieval of a past state? Is the recollection potentially defeasible while the act of remembering remains present?"
        * Failure Case: Reporting a past event using the present-access prat morpheme.
        3. te (Testimony/Attribution)
          * Logic Gate: "Is the information provenance localized in a third-party statement, document, or external record?"
            * Failure Case: Using prat for data found in a report; the report is the source (te), not the event.
            4. sa (Inference/Basis)
              * Logic Gate: "Does the conclusion require a premise-to-conclusion step that extends beyond the immediate data?"
                * Failure Case: Labeling a direct, present sensation as "inferred" merely because it is subjective.
                5. nu (Unidentified/NULL)
                  * Logic Gate: "Does the cognitive stack contain a retrieval path for the informational provenance? If NULL, apply nu."
                    * Failure Case: Using nu when the source is known but the confidence is low.

                    5. Integrated Endorsement Tier and Frame Interaction

                    ESL v1.1 strictly separates Endorsement (confidence) from Evidence (source) and Frame (context).

                    The Four-Tier Endorsement Slot

                    * -che (Full Endorsement): Total commitment within the stated frame.
                    * -pe (Favored but Defeasible): The speaker favors the proposition but identifies a live possibility of error.
                    * -po (Possibility): The proposition is entertained as a non-favored potential.
                    * -un (Unsettled): Explicit suspension of judgment; recorded without assertion.

                    Frame-Endorsement Matrix and Compiler Constraints

                    Frames (ke, fi, ra, uf) define the rules of evaluation. In ESL v1.1, the interaction between the Unresolved Frame (uf) and Endorsement is a compiler-level constraint.

                    Frame   Endorsement Permission  Logic / Constraint
                    ke (Practical)  All (che, pe, po, un)   Shared everyday meanings.
                    fi (Experiential)   All (che, pe, po, un)   Lived sensations and appearances.
                    ra (Defined)    All (che, pe, po, un)   Evaluation bound to specific models.
                    uf (Unresolved) MANDATORY -un   Structural Requirement: Any endorsement other than -un in an uf frame is "Epistemically Illegal."

                    6. Speech Act Operators and Boundary Protocols

                    ESL v1.1 organizes illocutionary operators around intent, separating "Accountable Assertions" from "Personal Boundaries."

                    * ka (Assertion): Propositional presentation with accountability for provenance.
                    * ki (Inquiry): Requests information within a stated frame.
                    * se (Imagination): Supposition for exploration; no actual claim.
                    * o (Attention): Directive for a specific act of inspection.
                    * re (Relinquishment): Instruction to suspend a conceptual distinction.
                    * vo (Boundary): Performance of a personal boundary.

                    The Boundary Protocol (vo)

                    The vo operator is modular and functionally distinct. To reinforce that permission belongs inherently to the affected person, the vo operator truncates the 6-slot template. A boundary act requires only ROOT + ACTOR. It bypasses evidence and endorsement slots because a boundary is a performative act, not a descriptive report.

                    * Contrast: ka-prat-che-fi ("I am certain I do not want this") is an evidentiary report; vo ("I do not authorize this") is a boundary performance that requires no evidentiary defense.

                    7. Empirical Validation Framework

                    The ESL v1.1 architecture is a hypothesis-testing tool for cognitive science. Success is measured by the delta between "unconscious conflation" and "structural precision."

                    Future Empirical Study Protocol

                    1. Distinction Accuracy Test: Measuring the speaker's ability to maintain the separation between direct sensation (prat) and inference (sa) under time pressure. Success is quantified by the Delta between "Inferred Cause" and "Direct Sensation" usage in reporting complex scenes.
                    2. Conflation Error Reduction: Comparing ESL speakers vs. English speakers in identifying "mind-reading" fallacies (T02/T09 cases). Success is defined by the speaker’s consistency in using Manifestational Access (-në) for others’ states.
                    3. Memory vs. Experience Retention: Testing if the mandatory me vs prat distinction improves the accuracy of eyewitness reports. Success is measured by the reduction in "retroactive overwriting" of memories.

                    ESL v1.1 functions as a "Grammar of Attention," ensuring that human communication remains structurally anchored in the precise provenance of its claims.
